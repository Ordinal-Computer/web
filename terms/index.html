03The Service

3.1 What the Service does

The App runs on macOS and Linux. You direct it at a project folder on your own device. When Boost is enabled, work you would otherwise execute locally, such as renders, exports, builds and test suites, is transmitted to Ordinal Machines and executed there instead. Files from your project are transferred so that those machines can execute the Job, and Results are written back into your local project folder. The Service is delivered as an application and not as a command line tool.

3.2 Two operational behaviours

Two behaviours are stated here because other provisions depend on them:

  • Only changed files are transferred. After the first run, the Service transfers the parts of your project that have changed since the previous run.
  • Repeat work may be answered from a cache. Where the same work has already been performed, the stored Result may be returned without executing the Job again. Section 11.4 governs caching and deduplication.

3.3 Boost is a control you operate

Boost is a switch, and you operate it. With Boost off, the Job executes on your own device and the files and command for that run are not transmitted to Ordinal. That is the control available to you, and it is the plainest one: work you do not boost does not leave your machine through Ordinal. That statement is narrower than “nothing leaves your machine”, because the App may remain signed in to your Account and may still report a crash. The Privacy Policy sets out what that leaves. Before you boost material that is sensitive or subject to a duty of confidence, inspect the switch rather than assuming its position.

3.4 No guarantee of completion or fallback

Ordinal does not represent, warrant or undertake that a Boosted Job will start, progress or complete, or that it will fall back to local execution if it does not. If a Boosted Job stalls, fails or queues longer than you are willing to wait, run it again, with Boost disabled where local completion is required. You are solely responsible for retaining your own copies of anything you are not willing to lose.

3.5 Supported platforms and executable work

Supported platforms at the date of these Terms are macOS on Apple silicon and Linux on x86‑64 and ARM. Builds for other platforms may appear on a releases page before they are supported, and anything not listed here is unsupported. Not every command can be executed remotely. Work requiring hardware attached to your own device, interactive work, and work that runs only on an operating system the Ordinal Machines do not run will remain local or will fail.
